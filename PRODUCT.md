# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

**Buyers (primary).** Venue and hospitality operators, event organisers, retail and area managers, facilities and property managers, and office managers across Manchester and the North West. They often arrive under time pressure: a weekend event needs licensed door staff, a store has a theft problem, a site needs cover tonight. Many check the site on a phone, outside office hours. Their job: get licensed, reliable officers on site, sometimes at short notice, from a company they can reach and hold to account.

**Applicants (secondary).** SIA licence holders and people working towards one: door supervisors, event and static officers. Mobile-first, usually in the evening. Their job: find steady, fairly paid shifts with a professional employer.

**Brand users (the brand page).** Spike office staff building quotes, rotas and email signatures; uniform and signage suppliers who need print-ready artwork; clients and partners who list Spike on their own material.

## Product Purpose

The site wins enquiries and applicants by proving, quickly, that Spike Security is licensed, reliable and reachable. Success is a buyer calling or sending an enquiry within one visit, and an applicant applying without needing to ask a question first.

The brand page is the single source of truth for how Spike looks and sounds, with every approved asset ready to download. It exists so the brand is settled in one place before the rest of the site is updated to match it.

## Brand Personality

**Calm. Visible. Accountable.**

- **Calm**: composed under pressure, never alarmist. Officers de-escalate; the brand does too.
- **Visible**: presence is the product. Security works by being seen, so the brand is clear and easy to spot rather than loud.
- **Accountable**: named people, real numbers, written reports. Every claim can be checked.

Voice is plain British English: short sentences, specifics over adjectives ("two SIA-licensed door supervisors from 9pm" beats "world-class security solutions"). Emotional goal for a buyer: relief that this is handled. For an applicant: respect.

Anchor references:
- UK road and safety signage (Kinneir and Calvert): legible at a distance, nothing decorative.
- Hi-vis workwear and rail-worker orange: orange as a working colour that keeps people safe, not as decoration.
- The GOV.UK style guide: plain, specific, accountable language.

## Anti-references

- "Tactical" security branding: skulls, eagles, crosshairs, camouflage, "elite" or "military-grade" language.
- Navy-and-gold heraldic crests (the retired v1 look).
- Stock photos of men in suits with earpieces, or door staff with folded arms.
- Black-and-yellow hazard tape everywhere, and anything that could be mistaken for the police.
- Dark-mode SaaS styling: neon glows, glassmorphism, gradient text.
- Fear-based copy ("Don't become the next victim").
- Dashboard theatre: status lights, "live" readouts or numbers that are not real.

## Design Principles

1. **Presence over noise.** Be seen clearly, then say less. One focal point per surface.
2. **Specifics earn trust.** Licences, response times, names and numbers beat adjectives every time.
3. **Built for the night shift.** Every asset has to work on a jacket under a streetlight, a sign across a car park and a phone in the rain.
4. **Honest by default.** No invented testimonials, fake live data or claims we cannot evidence.
5. **One brand, every surface.** The website, a hi-vis tabard and an email signature should be recognisably the same company.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.2 AA as the floor for everything on screen. Body text at least 4.5:1, large text and UI at least 3:1.
- Never rely on orange alone to carry meaning; pair it with text or shape.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; no motion is required to understand anything.
- Touch targets at least 44 × 44 px; the site is used one-handed on phones, often outdoors.
- Plain English for readers whose first language is not English, which includes many applicants.
- Print and signage: high contrast and large type for reading at distance and in low light.
